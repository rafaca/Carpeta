01 / Creative

The Reference Is the Password

Zoë scrolled past the same Margiela-for-Hermès reference three times in a week — three newsletters, three angles, functionally the same post. References once meant slow accumulation: years inside an agency, a monograph tracked down. Now they're indexed on Substack and downloadable in an afternoon, yet the industry still prices them as if they were scarce.

"Taste is what you recognise. Position is what you defend."Zoë, Art Direction

The Row, Toteme, and Khaite all pull from the same Phoebe Philo Céline lineage and produce entirely different work — because position, a defensible view held in public, is the differentiator. You cannot scrape a position off a Pinterest board. link

02 / Markets

The Furniture of a Market

A market clears when price compresses dispersed information into a signal. Most of what gets called a market doesn't. Enterprise software, higher education, defence, and professional services have all the furniture: prices, RFPs, forecasts — but the price is no longer doing the work.

"Build a better mousetrap, and the procurement officer will book the meeting with the incumbent's regional account manager — because that's whose name is on the email chain from last year."Studio Self

The buyer's decision optimizes for a defensible narrative: drop your price 40% and you'll be flagged as suspiciously cheap; win on a feature matrix and it gets filed in a drawer. The procurement officer's career depends on the deal not looking like a mistake — a very different problem from the deal being good. link

03 / Fashion

Brand Heat, Revenue Drop

Chanel debuted at #1 on Lyst's Q1 2026 index, pulling from 160 million shoppers. Matthieu Blazy put bags back at the center: pumps landed #2, the Maxi Flap #6, after recalibrating the pricing question that had hung over the maison.

Gucci climbed four spots to #5 — Demna's Milan debut pushed demand up 12% day-on-day. But Kering reported Gucci revenues down 14% in Q1 to €1.35B. Tapestry (Coach) is quietly closing the gap with Kering on market cap, a flip unthinkable a decade ago — INTAKE's read is that Gucci is winning attention while Coach is winning wallets. link

04 / Art

Power Licenses Appropriation

Arthur Jafa and Richard Prince are sharing a stage at Venice Biennale this week — 50+ works and a collaborative zine built from images they swapped during production. Both made careers from appropriation: pulled from social media, album covers, YouTube clips, celebrity memorabilia.

"More than permission, power licenses the act of appropriation."Jenny Wu, ArtReview

Their legal outcomes split: Jafa won the 2019 Golden Lion for a film made almost entirely of appropriated footage and has never been sued; Prince paid $900K in 2024 in the Instagram screenshots case. Jenny Wu in ArtReview names what the curatorial framing avoids: the show title is "Helter Skelter," and its organizing logic is the same. link

05 / AI

Doom Building

David Hoang found himself checking agent outputs between omakase courses — not social media, agents. It felt like a productive high with similar effects: by the time he sat down to write something that mattered, the reservoir was empty. His frame: Human-in-the-Loop means the system waits for your input; Human-on-the-Loop means it runs and you review on a schedule. Hoang moved most work to HOTL with a real 4pm review block, and kept HITL only for design and writing where he needs his fingerprints on it — not just his approval. link
