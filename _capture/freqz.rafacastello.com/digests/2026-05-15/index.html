01 / Fashion / Institution

What the Blockbuster Funds

The V&A's 2024–25 annual report logged a 50% surge in gross retail sales and an 84% jump in e-commerce, both tied to its Gabrielle Chanel exhibition. In 2025, the Louvre staged its first fashion show; 1.059 million visitors made it the museum's second most visited exhibition in 269 years.

"The blockbuster fashion show is both a lifeline and a symptom: it helps museums survive the funding crisis while drawing them deeper into the commercial logic that the public museum was once imagined to stand apart from."Ane Cornelia Pade, 1 Granary

Pade argues museums aren't choosing fashion out of curatorial enthusiasm. Government funding has stagnated, visitor numbers remain 13% below pre-pandemic levels, and fashion blockbusters drive tickets, memberships, venue hire, and merchandise simultaneously. The result: public institutions providing state-backed legitimacy to some of the world's most profitable companies. link

03 / Commerce / Strategy

The Organic Layer

Meta's customer acquisition cost is up 22% year over year; Google up 11%. At a London eComm summit, the broader message was consistent: paid reach is more expensive, and the organic layer — where someone else's audience trusts your brand — is where the edge compounds.

Affiliate revenue now traces 1 in 10 pounds at UK retail, creator investment grew 56% year over year, and partnership ads doubled. Hyperstudios points to Jacquemus (show invitations as collectibles) and NYX (product sent with zero creative direction) as the brands getting this right — ones that have built genuine creator relationships instead of running one-off placements. link

04 / Culture / Signal

The Room Next Door

A server who'd never heard of The Devil Wears Prada 2 prompted Scanclub to make the argument: the signal you're tracking obsessively may be invisible to the person two tables over. Cultural fragmentation is so advanced that "the whole world is watching" is almost never true.

"The future isn't happening everywhere, but it is happening somewhere. The only way to tell the difference between a real shift and your own echo chamber is to take what you're seeing out to dinner with you."Alyssa, Scan Club

ScanClub identifies a linked shift: constant news overload is causing people to disengage. Audiences are retreating toward newsletters and private group chats, actively protecting themselves from the flood. link

05 / Fashion / Style

Love Is Necessary, Insufficient

"Wear what you love" and "the most attractive thing you can wear is confidence" are, Blackbird Spyplane argues, big fat clichés that contain inarguable truths but illuminate nothing practical about how to dress. The problem: we are unreliable narrators of our own appearance, vulnerable to phenomena like pants dysmorphia (trousers you were certain were enormous three years ago now read as slim-fit).

Clothes are a social language. Other people are judges of what you look good in, not just you. Love and confidence are necessary for style — but rules, even ones you eventually reject, close the gap. link

06 / Media / Institution

Fifty Run the Culture

Four Hollywood studios. Three music companies controlling most hit songs. Five publishers controlling 80% of books. Ted Gioia argues the 2020s brought consolidation, not the promised democratization.

"The most scarce thing in media was trust. And if I could earn the trust of my reader, I would be rewarded, and they would be rewarded."Ted Gioia, The Honest Broker

The midlist is the casualty: publishers now require 50,000-copy commercial potential from debut authors, and studios won't greenlight profitable non-blockbusters. Gioia's prescription is parallel institutions — influencers becoming their own labels and publishers, building trust where institutional credibility no longer lives. link
