01 / Branding

Two Centuries Absent

Rice disappeared from Paestum for nearly two centuries. Mosquitoes and malaria had ended southern Campanian cultivation by the 1800s; modern drainage brought it back. Hera Nei Campi chose to frame that gap as identity.

Rosalba Multiverse put Hera's eye — goddess of fertility and seasonal cycles — as the central mark, classical references paired with a strong colour palette. The move lifts the product away from the neutral, interchangeable look of packaged rice. Lisa Cain at BP&O reads it as a brand that frames land, memory, and cultivation over pantry staple. link

02 / Industry

The Cost of Both

Five senior designers at luxury houses in Paris, Milan, and London spoke anonymously to 1 Granary about having children while working in-house at fashion brands.

"If you're a woman who wants a child and a great job, you really have to fight," says one creative director who returned from maternity leave to a superior screaming at her to change roles — and signed a confidentiality agreement after. Another was having contractions at five months pregnant and kept asking how she would finish the collection. The industry is female-dominated. It is not accommodating to mothers. link

03 / AI

Meeting Was the Toll

Brian Chesky lost steering of a 7,000-person Airbnb by 2019 and clawed it back through 35 hours of meetings a week. John Brewton's reading: the meeting was only ever the toll founders pay to see the company, and AI makes that toll near-zero.

The implication flips the hiring signal. Brewton argues the right hire now is a micro-CEO: execution-biased, technically capable across departments, owns outcomes and runs agents. Pure people managers become a red flag. Bayer already cut its management layers from twelve to six and removed close to 5,500 roles. link

04 / Strategy

Start From the Stranger

The homepage that opens with the founding story is an inside-out brief in action — meaningful context for someone already invested, noise to everyone who arrived cold. The Art of Branding calls the fix the stranger test: stop being the founder and become the person encountering the brand with zero context.

Outside-in starts from what that person needs to feel in order to choose you, then works backwards. Most briefs that produce forgettable work never make this turn: they hand a creative partner a mood board instead of a problem. link

05 / AI

Judgment Made Optional

Digital workers practiced scanning, clicking, and reacting for twenty years. The capacity to sit with a hard question eroded alongside it — until the skill was gone before anyone thought to protect it.

Making an Impact argues AI is about to replay that at scale. When the system drafts the summary and shapes the recommendation before the human enters the room, judgment becomes optional. Optional capabilities disappear from organizations before anyone realizes. The question each company needs to answer: which reasoning do you want people to still own in five years? link
