§ 01 The Belonging Question

Do I Belong in Tech Anymore?

Ky Decker’s essay in today’s Sidebar asks the question that a growing cohort of designers and engineers are whispering in DMs but not saying in standups: Do I belong in tech anymore? The subtitle frames the triptych: quitting, the spread of AI, and the loss of an ideal. It lands differently when paired with the week’s other data point about what “belonging in tech” now looks like from the inside.

That data point comes from The Pragmatic Engineer, who reports that developers at Meta, Microsoft, and Salesforce are deliberately burning AI tokens — and real money — to inflate their usage metrics. The practice has a name now: tokenmaxxing. Engineers are treating AI consumption targets the way they used to treat lines-of-code metrics: as a number to game, not a signal to learn from. The perverse incentive is structural. If your performance review measures how many tokens you consumed, you consume tokens. Goodhart’s Law applied to the AI stack.

“Devs are purposefully burning tokens (and money!) to inflate their AI usage and hit AI usage metrics which they treat as targets.” — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer

Meanwhile, from crixu.blog: “AI reads my meetings. I take more notes than ever.” The paradox is instructive. AI transcription tools were supposed to replace note-taking. Instead, the author finds that AI-generated summaries make handwritten notes more valuable, not less — because the act of writing forces synthesis that machines can capture but cannot perform. The constraint has shifted from recording to understanding.

Chris Coyier said it in Sunday’s Sidebar: “Raw coding speed isn’t the bottleneck. Alignment is.” Whether you’re a designer questioning your place, an engineer gaming your token count, or a PM watching AI transcripts pile up unread — the underlying crisis is the same. The tools got faster. The judgment didn’t.

§ 02 The AI Platform Shuffle

When Everyone Sells Everyone

The AI platform map reshuffled overnight. Amazon now offers OpenAI models on AWS Bedrock — the same platform that hosts Anthropic’s Claude and Amazon’s own Nova. The deal, confirmed by a joint TechCrunch and Stratechery interview with OpenAI and AWS leadership, means enterprise customers can now access GPT alongside Claude in a single cloud console. The moat is no longer exclusivity — it’s distribution.

At the other end of the spectrum: Google expanded Pentagon access to its AI systems after Anthropic declined the contract. The framing matters. Google, which once saw employee walkouts over Project Maven, is now the defense-sector backstop for AI capabilities that its competitor chose not to provide. Every AI company’s stated values will be tested by who their customer is, not what their about page says.

Snapchat introduced AI-powered conversational ads — interactive ad experiences that users talk to rather than scroll past. Amazon separately launched an audio Q&A feature for product pages where shoppers can ask questions by voice. Apple introduced a budget-friendly App Store subscription tier to expand developer accessibility. The common thread: every platform is adding an AI conversational layer, whether you asked for one or not.

And from the financial side: OpenAI reportedly missed its own sales and user targets ahead of its anticipated IPO (Yahoo Finance). Oracle and CoreWeave rushed to signal support. The gap between OpenAI’s valuation narrative and its revenue reality is the most interesting tension in the AI market right now. Not whether the technology works — but whether the business does.

§ 03 Tools & Craft

Building Accessible by Default

A11Y.MD surfaced on both Sidebar and Hacker News today: a context system for building accessible software by default, designed for developers and AI agents alike. It provides enforceable, WCAG-aligned rules that can be embedded directly into a project repository. The clever part: it works with AI coding assistants, so accessibility constraints propagate through AI-generated code — not despite it. The right response to “AI doesn’t know about accessibility” is not “don’t use AI” but “give AI the rules.”

Karl Koch on choosing the right tool: pick based on the project’s requirements, not your past preferences. Simple advice, rarely followed. Every team has a default stack that persists long after the project that justified it. Koch’s argument: defaults are a form of debt.

§ 04 World & Markets

World & Markets

The Fed is expected to hold rates steady as Chair Powell’s term nears its end — a monetary policy holding pattern that gives the earnings cycle room to breathe. And breathe it did: GM topped estimates and raised its profit forecast, buoyed by a Supreme Court ruling that reduces tariff-related costs. Starbucks surged on an earnings beat that exceeded both revenue and earnings expectations. T-Mobile raised its annual forecast on strong subscriber additions.

  • Logistics UPS cuts Amazon deliveries to boost margin — A structural decoupling from its biggest client. Amazon has been building its own logistics network for years; UPS is now openly de-risking the dependency. The endgame: two parallel delivery networks in the US.
  • Chips Chip stocks’ hottest winners are suddenly the weakest links — The semiconductor cycle is doing what it always does: punishing the stocks that ran fastest. The AI capex buildout is real, but the multiples priced in perfection.
  • Mobility Waymo expands to Portland — Another city, another autonomous deployment. The expansion is quietly methodical while the EV/autonomy discourse remains loudly polarized.
  • Privacy “Your phone is about to stop being yours” — 531 points on Hacker News, 286 comments. The most resonant tech-policy story of the day. When platforms control the hardware, the software, and now the AI layer, the ownership question becomes existential.
  • Security AISLE discovers 38 CVEs in OpenEMR — Critical vulnerabilities in healthcare software used by thousands of providers. A reminder that the most consequential security work is in the systems no one thinks to audit.
  • Security GitHub RCE vulnerability: CVE-2026-3854 — Remote code execution in the platform that hosts most of the world’s open-source code. Wiz published the breakdown. Patch status: active.
