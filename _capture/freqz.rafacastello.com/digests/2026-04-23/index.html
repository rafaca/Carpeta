Design & AI

When AI Designs Back

Anthropic launched Claude Design this week, and the design community is processing what it means. Describe what you need, Claude builds a first version. Refine through conversation, inline comments, or direct edits. If your team has a design system, Claude reads it and applies your tokens, components, and brand automatically. The handoff to Claude Code is built in — straight from design to production.

Sidebar led yesterday with Sam Henri Gold's reaction, noting "the beast is hairy enough that entire design roles now specialize in wrangling the system itself." Today's edition surfaced a complementary provocation from Dave Rupert: "I don't want a screenshot of your Claude conversation." The number of screenshots of Claude conversations is going up in his life, he writes, and it's beginning to have an impact on his general mood. The AI output is becoming a new form of social currency — one that substitutes process for taste.

Also from today's Sidebar: Matt Webb at interconnected.org argues for headless everything for personal AI — headless services are quicker and more dependable for personal AIs than having them click around a GUI with a bot-controlled mouse. The implication: the next wave of software might not have an interface at all. And from tante.cc, a provocation titled "AI as a Fascist Artifact" — arguing that our world is built around technological infrastructures that define what we see, who we can talk to, and what information gets presented to us.

AI attacks our identity in a way that most software doesn't. Unlike previous technologies, AI creates existential anxiety about job replacement. Ant Murphy, "The AI Chasm"

Ant Murphy's "The AI Chasm" punctured the adoption hype: McKinsey says 88% of organizations claim to use AI, but only about 1% have mature deployments delivering real value. Most usage is free chat interfaces, not integrated solutions. His advice to product leaders: reinvent yourself internally first — back-office efficiency before customer-facing features — and expect the real adoption curve to take 5–10 years.

On the other side of the AI coin, Mozilla used Anthropic's Claude to identify and fix 271 Firefox bugs — a practical demonstration of AI as security infrastructure rather than creative threat.

Culture & Criticism

Pricing the Unpriceable

JA Westenberg published a sweeping essay arguing that prediction markets are "the clearest single sign our civilisation has entered a late and decadent stage." Not because they're new or sinister — but because the case for them is so defensible while their long-term effect is corrosive. The technology works. The outputs are useful. And the civilizational cost is invisible until it isn't.

The historical arc is sharp: in 2003, DARPA's Policy Analysis Market — letting traders bet on assassinations and coups — was killed within 24 hours and ended Admiral Poindexter's career. By 2026, Polymarket lists contracts on the deaths of named public figures, the outcomes of active military operations, and nobody holds a press conference. We cite them in the Financial Times.

What a society chooses to price reveals what it actually gives a shit about, in the same way that what a society chooses to memorialise reveals what it honours. JA Westenberg

Westenberg threads the argument through Michael Sandel (What Money Can't Buy), Alasdair MacIntyre (After Virtue), James Scott (Seeing Like a State), and Joseph Tainter (The Collapse of Complex Societies). The through-line: when a civilisation loses its ability to answer "what should we do," it retreats to answering "what will happen?" Late Romans did it. Late medieval astrologers did it. Social Darwinists did it. Prediction markets are the 21st-century version of that surrender — converting questions of value into questions of fact, then trading the facts.

When ChatGPT prices the thinking stage

On the same day, Adweek published a leaked pitch deck revealing that StackAdapt has partnered with OpenAI to sell ad placements inside ChatGPT. CPMs range from $15 to $60, driven by prompt relevance rather than cookies or behavioral targeting. The minimum spend sits between $100K and $200K. Ads appear only to free-tier users and won't influence answers.

Search advertising was built on intent — catching people right before they act. ChatGPT ads target people earlier, at the thinking stage, when they're still forming opinions. If Westenberg's prediction markets price the future itself, ChatGPT ads price the moment just before a decision is made. The pattern is the same: another thing that was embedded in deliberation gets a number attached to it.

Power & Business

The Crown Changes Hands

Apple announced that Tim Cook will step down as CEO on September 1, becoming executive chairman. His successor: John Ternus, currently SVP of Hardware Engineering. Ternus is a 25-year Apple veteran who's been instrumental in defining the products — from iPhone to AirPods — that made Apple a $4 trillion company. Under Cook's 15 years: revenue up 303%, profits up 354%, market cap from $297B to over $4 trillion.

Ben Thompson argued that Cook's departure is prudent timing for both his legacy and the company's future. But two shadows loom — China dependency ("the biggest violation of the Tim Cook doctrine") and Apple's decision to white-label Google's Gemini for Siri, potentially ceding the AI race for good. With Ternus being a hardware engineer at heart, the question is where Apple's creative and commercial energy flows next — and whether the company that defined consumer tech can also define the AI era.

Meta dethrones Google

For the first time in two decades, Google is losing its crown. The Wall Street Journal reports that Meta is projected to surpass Google as the world's largest digital advertising business in 2026 — $243.46 billion vs. $239.54 billion. Meta's growth rate: 24.1% year over year, fueled by Reels ad performance and Advantage+ AI targeting. Google's 11.9% growth is weighed down by $20 billion in annual payments to Apple just to stay the default search engine. Together with Amazon, the three platforms now control 62.3% of all global digital ad spending.

More power shifts

e.l.f. Beauty promoted CMO Kory Marchisotto to President of e.l.f. Brands — a signal that the next phase of growth requires a marketer driving the brand portfolio while a dedicated AI officer handles infrastructure. The brand has now seen 28 consecutive quarters of growth, with net sales projected at $1.61 billion.

Microsoft is restructuring Xbox under new CEO Asha Sharma, scrapping the "Microsoft Gaming" umbrella and bringing Xbox back as its own entity — a bet that the brand name still carries more weight than the corporate parent. And Amazon committed another $5 billion to Anthropic (total: $13 billion), with Anthropic pledging to spend $100 billion on AWS over the next decade. Claude is now the only frontier AI model available across all three major cloud platforms: AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure.

Design & Making

Craft as Statement

Publix and creative agency FIG launched "Best Friends," a 360 campaign built around the idea that food can communicate what words can't. The hero film follows a group of neighborhood dogs trying to make amends with their beloved mailman, Bob — brought to life using fully customized, handmade puppets. No AI was involved. At a moment when AI-generated content is everywhere, choosing handmade puppetry says a lot about brand values. Craft and intentionality can be differentiators in and of themselves.

Core77's weekly roundup reinforced the argument for physical craft. The highlights: a full-length vertical jewelry cabinet on wheels from Thailand — a furniture typology rarely seen in the West. A concept CD player designed with Teenage Engineering in mind, by industrial designer Pierrick Piechaureaux. And Thumbimpression's Bamboo Sangat Chair, which expertly negotiates between material constraint and structural ambition.

Craighill's cocktail shaker spout was the week's best design case study — a good industrial designer asking the simplest question: "Why is this thing designed like that? Shouldn't it work better?" Also: Ralf Jacobs' incredible Harmonograph, furniture that can draw patterns. And the ICFF is coming to Javits Center, May 17–19.

OFFF Barcelona 2026 wrapped its edition: 5,500 daily attendees from 76 countries, plus 10,000 across three nights for The Screen mapping show. Still gives goosebumps.

Campaigns & Culture

Brand Plays Worth Studying

The LA Rams released a short film called "Thursday" as part of their 2026 NFL Draft rollout — a scene-for-scene parody of Ice Cube's 1995 cult classic Friday. It stars O'Shea Jackson Jr. (Ice Cube's real-life son) and Destin Tucker (Chris Tucker's son), stepping into the iconic roles their fathers played. Terry Crews cameos as a Deebo stand-in riding a beach cruiser flanked by Rams linebackers. Casting the actual sons made this campaign shareable for reasons beyond what they're promoting — a strong example of a brand showing up to a tentpole moment in a genuinely unique way. (ESPN)

OMO and Arsenal Women's Football Club launched "It Starts Outside," tackling the growing gender activity gap in children's outdoor play. Australian girls aged 4–11 are significantly less likely to play freely outside than boys the same age. This gap compounds over time and shows up directly in sport participation rates. The creative features childhood photos of Arsenal's Australian stars. The specificity of the message — pinning the conversation to a concrete behavior — helps it stand out from broader statements about girls in sport.

The media landscape rearranges itself

The Onion closed a deal to turn Alex Jones' Infowars into a full comedy network — possibly the most poetic brand transition of the year. Meanwhile, QVC's bankruptcy signals that TikTok Shop has eaten live shopping's original inventor alive.

And at Unwell, Alex Cooper's media company hired Pinterest veteran Joanne Bradford as president — but the move comes alongside a Bloomberg investigation reporting high employee turnover, a struggling slate of shows outside Call Her Daddy, and allegations of staff mistreatment on set. The Bradford hire reads less like a strong step forward and more like a stabilization play.

Adobe unveiled CX Enterprise at its annual Summit — an agentic AI system that monitors signals, recommends next-best actions, and manages cross-channel experiences automatically. Adobe is betting that the answer to AI disruption isn't fighting AI-native tools but becoming the orchestration layer between them. The stock, however, has shed roughly 30% of its value in 2026.

Geopolitics & News

World & Markets

United States

Ten people were shot at the Mall of Louisiana in Baton Rouge after two groups opened fire inside the mall; two are in critical condition. Suspects fled. Separately, the Senate advanced a $140 billion plan to fund ICE and Border Patrol, clearing a go-it-alone path for immigration enforcement spending. And the federal government is set to loosen marijuana rules in a major shift for the $47 billion industry.

Strait of Hormuz

Trump threatened to shoot boats laying mines as tension escalates in the Strait of Hormuz. Chinese spy satellites are reported to have been used by Iran to target US bases in the region — a 2024 deal that involved Chinese satellite intelligence for Iranian military targeting. Oil exporters are scrambling for alternative shipping routes, but as CNBC reports, there are no easy options.

Europe & Global

The EU approved a €90 billion loan for Ukraine after Hungary lifted its veto. Russian oil flows through the Druzhba pipeline resumed to Slovakia. The UK and France signed a £662 million deal on Channel crossings. Tanzania reported at least 518 deaths from post-election violence.

Supply chain signals

Condom prices could rise 30% due to the Iran war, says Karex, the world's top producer (5 billion condoms annually, supplying Durex, Trojan, and the NHS). Meanwhile, the Washington Post reports that students are speeding through online degrees in weeks, alarming educators about the devaluation of credentials.
