01 / AI Media

Readers Can't Tell

The Leverage ran 3,229 Substack posts through AI detection and found a flat line: posts flagged as 100% AI-generated get about as many reader reactions as fully human ones. Correlation: -0.005, statistical zero.

But the finding splits by category. Tech, Finance, and Business run 25–32% AI-flagged; Food, Art, and Politics hold at 3–9%. AI eats information categories — where readers pay for data they didn't already have — and leaves voice categories largely alone, where the person is the product. link

02 / Design

Handwriting Won't Hold

Brands are reaching for messy handwriting as a signal that a human made the work. A Weezer festival poster drawn in marker, Picador's prison-tattoo Bolaño reissues, Charli XCX's New York event announced with a handwritten note, Guess's January rebrand in acid-yellow lowercase.

"AI is a force multiplier of mediocrity."Max Ottignon, Ragged Edge

Kyle Chayka covers the wave in the New Yorker, reading it as a direct response to AI-generated images flooding feeds. The catch, which Chayka also notes: a viral ChatGPT prompt producing clumsy MS Paint-style drawings proved AI can already fake lo-fi on demand. link

03 / Brand

Designed to Be Shared

Pop Mart's 2024 revenue hit $1.81 billion, doubling year-over-year, with international sales up more than 700%. Labubu, a character licensed from Hong Kong artist Kasing Lung, drove most of it.

The breakthrough came when Blackpink's Lisa was photographed with a Labubu keychain; Pop Mart was monitoring social and commerce signals in real time and reallocated resources immediately. A key product decision followed: moving Labubu into a soft vinyl plush format after data showed tactile figures generated more social sharing than hard-shell ones. Figures retailing at $13–16 were soon reselling for $700. link

04 / Opportunity

No Brand Layer

Pickleball is a $2 billion market growing toward $9 billion by 2034 with no brand layer built. Selkirk Sport did nearly $100 million last year on paddles alone, but apparel stays trapped: Recess, a lifestyle brand in the space, pulled $1.2 million.

Creative Weapons argues the opportunity is in accessories. Tifosi Optics does $20 million in sport sunglasses; Titan sells ball machines at $2,400 each. The bag play, the eyewear play, the training-tool ecosystem — all of it sits open in a sport where most players still wear what they already own. link

05 / Culture

Against Self-Promotion

Letty Cole, founding editor of Lost Property and former strategist at MØRNING, argues in Post Culture that personal brand is corroding the creativity it's meant to promote. The pressure to commercialize every inch of the work is relentless, made worse by validation-fuelled dopamine loops.

Artful self-promotion is a real skill, Cole says, but most creatives end up making decisions from ego rather than art. Separately, she reads the influencer economy as at an inflection: conceptually exhausted, yet structurally too efficient for brands to walk away from. link
