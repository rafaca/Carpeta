01 / Brand Design

Heritage as Raw Material

Pentagram's wordmark for St Paul's Cathedral traces to a letterform found in the Cathedral's own library: an elevated 'S' and 'T' that solves the apostrophe problem without a workaround.

Domenic Lippa's team worked with the Cathedral's own stonemasons to cut the mark in stone. Color palette from hues already in the building; primary typeface Arizona Flare by Dinamo. The identity, BP&O argues, sits at exactly the right position on the religious-secular dial for each viewer — unmistakably St Paul's, without declaring its hand. link

02 / Fashion Business

Slowly, Then All at Once

Christopher Shannon's advice to the next generation of designers skips the catwalk: "Focus on how you think your point of view can be employed and monetised." The traditional system, he says, is "dying before our eyes."

1 Granary gathered 18 independent London designers on what they'd do differently. The consensus: skip wholesale, build direct-to-consumer from day one, and move slowly. Luke Derrick's note to himself: "FOMO is short-lived. The industry is too fast for anyone to remember last week's status quo." link

03 / AI Design

Four Jobs, One Label

"AI design" is one label but has forked into four distinct orientations, and most design teams are only staffed for one of them.

Sarah and Kate map the split: designing with AI (using it in your workflow); designing AI products and interfaces; structuring content so AI agents can navigate it; and shaping how a model behaves and speaks. Most teams have zero coverage in the last two. Their argument: those decisions are being made by engineers, by default. link

04 / Consumer Culture

Whimsy's Price

Etsy searches for whimsy-related items are up at least 50% year over year. Harvard psychiatry professor Nassir Ghaemi ties the surge to information overload and exhaustion with performative online life.

Lowe's bet on it: MrBeast-themed in-store kids workshops, Scout-style badge earning, $14.98 buildable toy kits, royalties for Donaldson. CMO Jen Wilson: "Gen Alpha actually has a larger influence on where their parents shop than any other influence." The TikToker rebuttal: "That's capitalism." link

05 / Work

Company of One

29.8 million US solopreneurs generate roughly $1.7 trillion annually. Sequoia now underwrites what it calls "agentic leverage" — one or two operators producing the output of a 10-person team.

John Brewton traces the arc from medieval commons through 2008 (self-employment absorbed 45% of UK employment growth post-crisis) to AI carrying the cost curve the rest of the way down. His warning: platform dependency looks more like feudalism than freedom. Brewton's answer is direct ownership: email lists and relationships alongside every platform bet. link

Design Changing from Artifact-Production to Intent-Shaping

AI isn't making designers faster at producing screens — it's changing the object of design itself, shifting the most valuable UX contribution from artifacts to encoding judgment into live systems.
