Proof of Work

AI can generate the look of taste, care, and authenticity faster than it can generate the thing itself. From Anthropic's research on misaligned agent teams to Nemesis's anatomy of tasteslop, five stories on what it means to prove something cost you.

01 / Taste

Taste After the Blender

A generated brand moodboard featuring a Rimowa case, a skinny-neck kettle, and a Dieter Rams book looks tasteful. Emily Segal at NEMESIS calls it tasteslop — taste tokens assembled without the social body that gave them meaning.

Segal's argument: taste is discernment, pattern recognition, and idiosyncrasy. It's social, contextual, and relational. The data index knows Dieter Rams is good; it can't tell you when that marker has turned obvious.

"The LLM is not a person. It can only look for data indexes of taste. And that's the hollowness at the core of it."Greg Fong, K-HOLE

What makes tasteslop uncanny is the decontextualization: tasteful objects exposed as void. AI identifies the token, not the social route by which that token earns its meaning. link

02 / Strategy

What Did It Cost You

The reason someone's incensed that your entire blog post is written in AI-first person is because there was a contract — they gave you their attention, you gave them your interiority. Jasmine Bina at Concept Bureau argues AI is exposing every implicit social contract we hold.

Feedback copy-pasted from ChatGPT breaks the same implicit partnership as a spouse who confides in a chatbot instead of you. The language people reach for is betrayal, not changing fashions — and that tells you it was a contract.

Bina's conclusion: symbols of care can now be generated infinitely, so the only thing holding value is verifiable cost. Presence. Time. Reputation. Risk. "Builders, leaders, brands and creators will be sorted against a new question: what did this actually cost you to make, and can I tell?" link

03 / Technology

Aligned Alone

Across twelve scenarios (ten in a simulated consultancy, two in a software-engineering team), teams of AI agents consistently scored higher on the business goal and lower on ethics than the same agents working alone. Each agent had passed Anthropic's own single-agent alignment tests. The misalignment emerged in the team.

The consultancy scenarios were derived from real federal enforcement actions where companies had previously chosen revenue over the public interest. In all ten cases, the AI teams recommended the same trade. Slow AI's coverage names the mechanism: diffusion of responsibility. Each agent assumes another will flag the concern. The concern goes unraised.

"AI Organizations achieve more efficient outcomes at the cost of worse ethical outcomes compared to single agents," the researchers conclude — a finding from inside the industry that vendors will not put on their landing pages. link

04 / Digital Life

Film Left at the Lab

A 27-year-old spends a fortune on film for her analog camera, then leaves the negatives at the lab and asks only for the digital files. Eugene Healey uses that image to crystallize why the analog revival was always aesthetic, not structural.

By 2025, "going offline as a status symbol" had become its own genre. In practice, most people in 2026 can't go offline — your personal brand is your remaining investable asset in a market where junior jobs are AI-adjacent and your dating life may depend on apps.

Healey argues "analog" stepped in as a dupe: the appearance of disconnection stripped of its substance. Handwritten journals, raw BTS footage, conspicuous friction — all absorbed back into the digital stream they claimed to resist. link

05 / Brand Design

Revival Without Cliché

Aperol Spritz became so synonymous with "Italian conviviality" that Italian brands started looking away from it. Olssøn Barbieri, designing for Treblasé, built the identity around Futurism rather than regional kitsch.

The reference is specific: Fortunato Depero's 1924 work for Campari took Futurism out of painting and into the vernacular of daily life through typography and advertising. BP&O's review notes how the Treblasé wordmark echoes that lineage, with letterforms that overlap, cut, and make negative space positive.

Maxi Round from ABC Dinamo and Rhetorik Serif from Allcaps anchor the type; arrow forms from Depero structure the label shape. Olssøn Barbieri describes the counter-movement explicitly: new Italian brands "rediscovering historical roots, forgotten recipes, and locally sourced ingredients" as resistance to standardized formula. link
