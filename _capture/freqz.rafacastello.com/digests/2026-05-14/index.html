01 / AI / Strategy

The Consensus Engine

Ask any major AI model for a random number between 1 and 10. GPT picks 7 ninety-two times out of a hundred; Claude, ninety; Gemini, a hundred. Strategy consultant Zoe Scaman used that finding to name the enterprise trap: every organization running the same reasoning engine gets back the same answers.

"The same intelligence, flooding everything. Every organisation drinking from the same well, getting back the same answers, all arriving at 7."— Zoe Scaman, Musings of a Wandering Mind

Scaman calls the asset at risk "heartwood" — the tacit knowledge and institutional judgment that accumulates over decades and can't be regrown. AI is the fastest thing a company has ever deployed; heartwood is the slowest. Organizations that protect it can build things no model can replicate. link

02 / Craft

An Archive Is Alive

Miss Deanna started in a home workshop in the late 1950s. By 1971, founder Deanna Ferretti Veroni had repositioned as technical partner to designers: a deliberate choice she calls the best of her career. Over the next 33 years she produced knitwear for Kenzo, Armani, Versace, and Margiela, choosing clients by a gallerist's rule — no two identities could overlap.

"If you only look within your own industry, you'll only ever find what already exists."— Deanna Ferretti Veroni, 1 Granary

Her method for Margiela's body-imprint sweater involved a flea market hat, a discontinued French flooring nylon, and a license plate factory's furnaces. Ferretti Veroni tells young designers: don't send emails to factories — go stand next to the machine. link

03 / Policy

The Artist's LLC

Colorado SB26-133, the Colorado Artist Company Act, passed the state House 49-14 on Monday and heads to Governor Polis's desk. It would create the first LLC structure in America designed specifically for creative people.

The A-Corp adds four protections unavailable in a standard LLC: the founding artistic mission is legally shielded; IP counts as a capital contribution to the entity's valuation; artists must hold at least 51% of all voting shares; and fractional ownership can be structured at formation. Out-of-state creators can register through Colorado's existing process, with first A-Corps expected by early 2027. link

04 / Music

The Slop Subsidy

60,000 fully AI-generated tracks are uploaded daily across streaming platforms. On Deezer, 85% of their streams are fraudulent — they sit in the royalty pool anyway, diluting per-stream payouts for every artist who built a real audience.

Joel Gouveia's redesign starts with two structural fixes. Require human identity verification at upload — the same KYC brokerage accounts already use. Replace pro-rata royalties with user-centric payments: your $11.99 goes only to artists you listened to, not a pool split by total platform streams. That structure subsidizes major label catalogs at every indie artist's expense. link

05 / Retail

The Meal, Not the Bag

Ven.Space, the buzziest menswear boutique near Prospect Park in Brooklyn, carries the right names — Comoli, Cristaseya, A.Presse — and has no web store, so everything must be encountered in person. That premise collapses under the store's own popularity: shelves so overloaded that Lemaire blends into Lady White.

The columnist at Big Pants argues that shopping for considered clothing should feel like eating a good meal: you see it, smell it, hold it, try it on, each step compounding the last. Ven.Space has fine-dining ingredients packed into a paper bag. link

06 / Education

Blue Books Return

Princeton's faculty voted last week to proctor exams for the first time since 1893, ending 133 years of self-policed honor. Academic dishonesty cases rose from 50 in 2021-22 to 82 last year. A senior survey found 30% admitted to cheating; 28% said they'd used ChatGPT on assignments where it was forbidden.

"As long as people think there is more cheating, it encourages more cheating."— Nadia Makuc, Princeton senior, via After School

Take-home exams fell more than two-thirds last year. Blue books are back; one professor now requires Google Docs to review composition history. Students discuss cheating openly on Fizz, the campus anonymous app — the norm compounds itself, says one senior, as long as people believe everyone else is doing it. link
