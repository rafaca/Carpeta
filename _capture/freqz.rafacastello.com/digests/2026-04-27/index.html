§ 01 Taste & Strategy

When Taste Becomes Strategy

Ana Andjelic’s “What Rick Rubin and Duomo Have in Common” in this week’s Sociology of Business opens a provocation that has been quietly spreading across the strategy Substacks: taste, not reach, is the durable competitive advantage. Rick Rubin gets inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame this November. His most important line, delivered to Anderson Cooper: he trusts his taste completely.

The case study is The Devil Wears Prada 2 — a film about the foremost taste-making industry in the world that almost certainly measured its entire promotional campaign in impressions, reach, and views. The celebrity cameos, the Vanessa Friedman coverage, the Kara Swisher involvement, the method red-carpet dressing, the Dior activations — all spectacle, all attention-optimized. Andjelic’s point: brands reorganized themselves around attention metrics despite this being the opposite of how cultural relevance is built.

“Impressions, reach, and views are not cultural relevance. They are evidence that people scrolled past you.” — Ana Andjelic, The Sociology of Business

What connects Rubin and the Duomo is patient, accumulated taste. The Duomo was built over 140 years. Rubin built his ear over decades. Neither could be A/B tested into existence. The tension Andjelic identifies: brands “talk about brand as culture” but optimize for the opposite. The implication is structural — taste requires a different organizational capability than reach, not just different creative direction.

That principle lands differently alongside Rockefeller’s line from Farnam Street’s Brain Food #678: “Wealth isn’t a distinction. If I have no other achievement to my credit than the accumulation of wealth, then I have made a poor success of my life.” Substitute reach for wealth and the parallel is exact: reach isn’t relevance. The number is not the thing.

Also in Farnam Street’s orbit this week: Shane Parrish’s Knowledge Project interview with Greg Brockman, OpenAI’s co-founder, on the Sam Altman firing, why they moved away from AI safety research, and what’s coming. Brockman’s most useful answer: what happens to your job when alignment — not speed — becomes the bottleneck.

Most 🔥 of the Week

The news items

InfoWars has been officially taken over by Global Tetrahedron, parent of The Onion, purchased during Alex Jones’s bankruptcy auction. Tim Heidecker is in charge. The new logo proudly flaunts the LGBTQ+ rainbow. Whatever InfoWars becomes, the rebrand is a rare case of brand identity as institutional reclamation — or institutional comedy, depending on your read.

The Washington Commanders introduced a “Hail Raiser” alternate uniform featuring a spear referencing Native American imagery — just six years after dropping the Redskins name under pressure. The Association on American Indian Affairs responded: “We are not your mascot.” The brand lesson: changing a name under pressure is not the same as changing a culture.

Font of the week: Alverata by TypeTogether, spotted in the Extellis identity by Hoodzpah. A textured serif that plays against satellite-tech expectations. And finally: Pickle Beer — PBR × Grillo’s Pickles, a limited-edition “easy-drinking, sessionable” dill-forward lager. Hard to compete with pickle beer for attention. The design delivers.

§ 03 Software & AI

Software Is Liquid

Jamie Thingelstad’s Weekly Thing 345 featured the essay of the week: “Software Is Liquid.” Originally a talk to technology leaders, now adapted for his newsletter. The central claim: software has historically been a solid — chiseled out of granite, expensive to change, which is why waterfall existed. With agentic development, software is becoming liquid. The economics have changed so profoundly that every paradigm built on “software is expensive” needs re-examination.

Waterfall existed because refactoring C was catastrophically expensive — you had to be right before you started. Agile emerged because scripting languages made refactoring cheaper. The cloud did the same for infrastructure. Now AI does it for the act of creation itself. Discovery should happen in code. Managers belong in code. Velocity estimates are broken — because the constraint is no longer the making, it’s the knowing what to make.

“We are building the best practices before they exist.” — Jamie Thingelstad, Weekly Thing

Claude Design launched from Anthropic Labs. Thingelstad tested it on the POAP KINGS site — strong on visual language understanding, integrates with Claude Code. GPT-5.5 and ChatGPT Images 2.0 dropped from OpenAI the same week. Cloudflare Email Service for agents entered public beta: email inboxes purpose-built for agents, not humans. Fastmail launched an MCP server — instant Claude integration, “super-simple” setup. Codex added computer-use features that Thingelstad calls the best he’s tested.

From Sidebar, Chris Coyier’s “AI & Alignment”: “Raw coding speed isn’t the bottleneck. Alignment is.” Teams produce PRs faster but don’t know what to build. His reference: 9 women can’t have a baby in 1 month. More agents in parallel does not solve the coordination problem. The constraint has shifted from execution to judgment.

§ 04 Web Design

The Web as Canvas

MaxiBestOf Week 17 picks lean into editorial restraint and typographic intelligence. The selections feel calibrated for a moment when the web is rediscovering craftsmanship after the AI-generated-everything wave. Four standouts:

Algorithm Fatigue

Data visualization site exploring the exhaustion of algorithmic recommendation. Typography: ABC Gravity by Dynamo + CoFo Sans. The combination of a heavy grotesque and a humanist mono creates productive tension with the subject matter.

Unreasonable Hospitality

Marketing site by Thread, using ABC Otto + Founders Grotesk by Klim. Manages to feel genuinely warm despite being a marketing site — a harder design problem than it sounds.

Nelson Byrd Woltz

Landscape architecture site by Say, set in Neue Haas Unica. Clean spatial hierarchy that lets the landscape photography breathe. The typographic restraint makes the images do the work.

Superculture

Agency site using Boogy Brut by Bureau Brut + Suisse Int’l Mono. Deliberately abrasive in the best way. The kind of site that knows exactly what it is and does not apologize for it.

Also from Sidebar this week

  • Tool What’s Your Attention Worth? — Enter your age, location, and screen time to see what the ad industry spent targeting you. Makes Ana Andjelic’s thesis tangible: attention has a specific dollar value. Taste doesn’t.
  • Tool Worse On Purpose — Corporate autopsies on the brands you trust. Who bought them, who gutted them, what’s still worth buying. The anti-thesis of taste: extraction.
  • Reads The Role of a New Machine — Dan Cohen uses an old book to put today’s new technology in perspective. Filed under Technology / UX / History. Sidebar pick of the week.
§ 05 World & Markets

World & Markets

The most jarring story of the week: Cole Tomas Allen, 31, was thwarted after opening fire at the White House Correspondents’ Dinner at the Washington Hilton. Guests dove to the floor. Secret Service agents climbed over tables to protect officials. Allen appeared in federal court days later. Garbage Day’s Ryan Broderick noted the most striking thing wasn’t the shooting itself but the country’s collective shrug. In 2024’s Butler assassination attempt, phones everywhere buzzed with alerts. This time, bars kept showing the Knicks game. Dana White: “It was fucking awesome.” The Atlantic argued the shooting “made clear who gets saved first.” Conspiracy theories flew from both sides within hours.

  • Policy DOJ reclassifies medical marijuana to Schedule III (6Pages) — Recreational still illegal, but Section 280E tax deductions could be transformative for the $47B industry where fewer than 25% of businesses are currently profitable. Effective tax rates of 70–80% could drop dramatically overnight.
  • Space/Tech Amazon Leo + Blue Origin challenging SpaceX/Starlink (6Pages) — $11.6B Globalstar acquisition, first New Glenn rocket reuse. The Bezos-connected ventures are starting to apply real competitive pressure where none existed a year ago.
  • Energy CATL + BYD ultra-fast EV charging (6Pages) — 6.5 minutes to near-full charge, approaching gas-car parity. 25%+ of new cars sold globally are now electric. The adoption curve is no longer theoretical.
  • Advertising Meta projected to surpass Google in ad revenue by end of 2026 (Reuters via 6Pages) — eMarketer data shows Meta pulling ahead globally. The platform consolidation story has a new chapter.
  • Energy New nuclear plants breaking ground (WSJ via 6Pages) — TerraPower (Bill Gates) in Wyoming and Kairos (Google-backed) in Tennessee. First new commercial nuclear plants in the US in over a decade. The Overton window on nuclear has moved decisively.
  • Media Warner Bros./Paramount deal approved; Peacock lost $11B+ (Bloomberg via How Long Gone) — Comcast’s streaming bet has been one of the most expensive in the consolidation era. The deal reshapes the legacy media map again.
  • Media Letterboxd controlling investor looking to sell (Semafor via How Long Gone) — One of the few genuine 2020s digital-media success stories — community-driven, profitable, beloved — may be changing hands. Worth watching who buys.
  • Startups SF dominates unicorn market cap (Elad Gil via Houck/Founding Journey) — 63% of post-2023 unicorn value vs 33% before. The COVID-era “SF is over” narrative is conclusively dead. The concentration is more extreme than the pre-COVID peak.
