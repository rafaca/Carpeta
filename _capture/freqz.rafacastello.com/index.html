01 / Identity Design

Heraldry Redrawn

Kit Studio's redesign of the coat of arms and shield system for Durham University St John's College (reviewed as "A Crested Development" on Brand New) earned 94.2% "Great" on shield variations — the highest vote-getter of the week and the standout image according to Armin Vit.

The wordmark scored 86.8% Great; applications 84.8%. Commenters called the work "stunning example of craft and design" and "Heavenly." In a week where no project cracked 25% Bad votes — not even Spotify's temporary disco ball icon — Kit Studio's heraldic system was the clear standout. link

02 / Fashion

Museum as Alibi

Louis Vuitton Cruise at the Frick Collection and Dior Cruise at LACMA follow the same brief, Ana Andjelic argues: borrow the symbolic authority institutions spent decades earning, then attach product pricing to it.

Ghesquière partnered with the Keith Haring Foundation for the LV show; Anderson used Ed Ruscha's slogans on Dior shirts now selling for thousands. This year's Met Gala exhibition, titled "Costume Art," was co-sponsored by Lauren Sanchez and Jeff Bezos. Andjelic argues the strategy creates commercial advantage — competitive differentiation, pricing justification, social distinction — but corrupts spaces built for public access to art. link

03 / Brand Design

The Opposite of Hype

Made Thought's identity for Juana, a Dubai-based CBD skincare brand, enters a category "oversaturated and loaded with false claims" by limiting everything: color to browns, greens, and yellows (including a nuclear yellow, Hex #E4E928), and typeface to a single face throughout.

That face is Rules by Blaze Type, a neo-grotesque with Swiss Modern roots and enough idiosyncrasy to carry a premium range. BP&O's reviewer finds Juana "feels established, trusted and bedded-in despite its relative newness" — the product of restraint in a field where most brands shout. link

04 / Brand Campaigns

Product as Plot Device

Hellmann's Garlic Aioli launched in 2023 and struggled to find its footing. The fix, via Edelman: the world's first garlic-scented novel — a special-edition of Jennifer Armentrout's vampire romantasy "The Primal of Blood and Bone," printed with garlic-infused ink and shipped in coffin-shaped mailers to creators.

The Bestsmeller campaign drove 39% weekly dollar sales growth within three weeks, 22.5% overall sales velocity, and 1.6 billion earned media impressions. The book hit #2 on the New York Times Bestseller list. Garlic's centuries-old vampire association gave Hellmann's a genuine cultural bridge into BookTok rather than just showing up where the audience was. link

05 / AI

The Frictionless Default

In a study of 758 Boston Consulting Group consultants (half with GPT-4 access), AI users vastly outperformed peers on most tasks but significantly underperformed on the one problem the AI was known to fail; most accepted the authoritative-looking wrong answer without catching it.

Turkish high school students using ChatGPT did better on homework, worse on actual tests. Taipei students with an AI tutor scored 0.15 standard deviations higher on a final exam — equivalent to six to nine months of additional schooling. Mollick argues the problem is design: agentic tools produce answers before users have decided whether to think. link
