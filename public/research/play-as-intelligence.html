Play is the oldest form of human intelligence

Why the instinct we outgrow first is the one we need most

Those Who Play · Research · July 2026

Before language, before tools, before the first fire — there was play. This paper argues that the behaviours we file under ‘childish’ are the operating system every other human capability boots from.

Play is how we learn, adapt, and connect. It reveals what logic hides, dissolves fear, and makes collaboration natural. Watch any group given a game and a deadline: the game wins, and the group is better for it.

We treat play as a serious tool for culture, learning and innovation — not a decoration applied after the strategy is done. When participation is designed as a game with real stakes, audiences stop spectating and start co-authoring.

This is the first in a series of working papers from the studio. It is a placeholder essay: the argument is real, the citations are coming.
